# Research question

**Can a tensegrity-based upper-limb exoskeleton give meaningful elbow assistance while staying comfortable, mobile, and controllable?**

Rigid exoskeletons are heavy and restrictive. Cable-driven ones are lighter, but they usually need a big external frame or a bulky pack of motors and batteries. There isn't much research on whether a tensegrity structure, with rods held in place by tensioned cables much like bones and muscles, can do the job while staying light and compliant.

My goals for the summer:

1. Make the inherited tensegrity arm actually wearable.
2. Add sensing and cable actuation so it can assist elbow flexion.
3. Narrow the use case to something testable: helping finish a biceps curl when the muscle starts to give out.
4. Measure what works and what doesn't.

Where it landed: the sensing and control loop works, but the motors aren't strong enough to give real assistance yet. See the [main README](../README.md) for results.
